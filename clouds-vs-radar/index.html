Clouds over the world's cities

An optical satellite sees the ground only under a clear sky. This page counts how often Sentinel-2 had one over 52 cities from 2023 to 2025. Radar satellites send their own microwaves, and clouds do not stop them. The page accompanies the post Radar, Explained #1.

        Why radar is different

        Sentinel-2 measures sunlight reflected from the ground. Cloud droplets scatter that light, so a cloud hides what is below it.

        NISAR's L-band radar transmits its own pulses. Their wavelength is many times longer than a cloud droplet is wide, so the droplets scatter almost none of the signal. The radar also works at night because it does not use sunlight.

        Heavy rain can weaken shorter radar wavelengths, such as X band.

        How this was measured

        For 52 cities, we took every Sentinel-2 L2A scene from 2023 to 2025. That is 20,980 scenes from the public Earth Search catalog (Element 84). We read the scene classification (SCL) in a 20 km square centered on each city. For each day we kept the scene with the most valid pixels, and left out days when fewer than half of the square was imaged. A pass is clear when at most 10% of the valid square is cloud, cirrus or cloud shadow (SCL classes 3, 8, 9, 10). The 30% and 50% buttons change that limit and nothing else.

        The world layer is a separate data set. It is ERA5 monthly mean total cloud cover, 2023 to 2025, on a 0.5 degree grid, averaged to 2 degree cells for this page and interpolated between cells for display.

        • Each count describes a 20 km square around the city center. Cloud elsewhere in the metropolitan area does not enter it.
        • The SCL classifier can err.
        • Sentinel-2 passes happen around 10:30 local time. Cloud at other hours does not enter the count.
        • ERA5 averages all hours of the day, so a cell on the world layer can differ from the city count beside it.
        • The longest gap is the time between two clear passes, or between a clear pass and an end of the record. A gap that touches an end of the record may be longer in reality.
        • The number of passes differs by city. The panel shows it for each city.
        • Monthly shares are rounded to whole percent. Each calendar month holds three years of passes.
