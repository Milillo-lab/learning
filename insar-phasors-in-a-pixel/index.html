Phasors in a pixel

One pixel of a radar image is not one echo. It is the sum of the echoes of every object inside the resolution cell, each an arrow in the complex plane. Move the objects, move the radar, let the leaves stir, and watch why the phase of one image is random while the phase difference of two images is not.

The idea in one equation

Each scatterer k at range Rk returns a phasor of amplitude ak and phase −4πRk/λ. The pixel stores their sum,

s = Σk ak exp(−j 4π Rk / λ)

A range difference of a quarter wavelength, 1.4 cm at C band, turns an arrow by half a turn, so the arrows of a cell a few meters wide point every which way. A second pass from a nearby position changes every range by almost the same amount: the whole chain turns as one piece, and the phase of s1s2* keeps the geometry. If the objects move between the passes, the chain is rebuilt and that phase is lost. The share of the signal that survives is the coherence.

Try this

  1. With no motion, drag the geometric phase from 0° to 360°. The chain in the middle panel turns as one piece, its length does not change, and every one of the 2,000 cells agrees on the phase. Why does the phase of s₁ alone tell you nothing, while the phase of s₁s₂* tells you the geometry?
  2. Select the roof edge and move it by λ/4. Predict first: by how much does its arrow turn, and does the pixel get brighter or darker? Now do the same with a branch. Why does the brightest object decide most of the answer?
  3. Set the motion to 0.5 cm at C band with only the vegetation moving. The histogram spreads, but a peak survives at the geometric phase. What sets the height of that peak? Uncheck the box so that everything moves, and compare.
  4. Keep the motion at 1 cm and switch from C band to L band. The same leaves, moving by the same amount, barely matter to NISAR. Find the motion at which L band loses as much coherence as C band did at 0.5 cm, and check it against the ratio of the wavelengths.
  5. Press "New arrangement" several times with no motion. The pixel value jumps around (speckle), yet the phase difference stays put. Which of the two is a property of the ground and which of the arrangement?
