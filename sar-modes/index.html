Stripmap

Where the gaps really are. In ScanSAR and TOPS the antenna footprint is longer than the distance the platform moves during one full sub-swath cycle, so consecutive bursts on a sub-swath overlap on the ground and the coverage is contiguous. What each target loses is time: it is recorded for only part of its footprint crossing, which shortens the synthetic aperture and coarsens azimuth resolution. SweepSAR has no bursts, but it does have gaps, in range: the radar cannot receive while it transmits, so each transmit event blanks a band of the swath. NISAR moves those bands by dithering its PRF so that the missing samples can be filled (Freeman et al. 2009; Villano et al. 2014; Kellogg et al. 2020).
